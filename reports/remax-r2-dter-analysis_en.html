ReMax R2 — In-house DTER Sweep

9 candidate ReMax checkpoints vs Qwen3.5-audio baseline · 18 in-house DTER datasets · 6 locales
Source data: remax_r2_combined_all_seg.xlsx (all-segment eval, bundled in _assets/)
Metric: DTER ↓ WERR = 1 − DTER/baseline Eval set: eval_inhouse_2605_all_seg 📥 Download xlsx
Problem:  9 reward/aggregation variants on top of ReMax R2 — which recipe actually moves the needle on in-house DTER?
Finding:  8 / 9 models improve on 18 / 18 datasets; best is fmt_judge mean@s200 at +1.61 % WERR.
Risk:  mean@s1000 collapses (DTER 0.20 → 0.50, nl-NL Conv jumps to 0.91) — long-horizon training without value-baseline guardrails is unstable.

🌍 Per-Language WERR (healthy 8)

  • hu-HU dominates the gains: every model gives +4.5 % to +6.9 % WERR — the locale where this training pipeline matters most.
  • en-US universally regresses by −0.5 % to −1.7 % — likely because the baseline is already strongest on English and the punctuation reward over-corrects.
  • fmt_judge is the only model that improves every locale (incl. nl-NL +1.0 %, cs-CZ +0.1 %); pure punctuation rewards regress on nl-NL or cs-CZ.
  • nb-NO: small but consistent +0.3 % to +1.3 % across all 8.

🧪 Dataset-Type Effect (Top-3 Models)

  • Dictation sees the biggest gain across all top-3 (+1.9 % to +3.5 %) — clean read-speech benefits most from punctuation/format rewards.
  • Conversation shows mixed results — the harder, more disfluent setting partly absorbs the gains.
  • OnlineMeetings is the hardest to move: sum,190 goes slightly negative, only fmt_judge nets a positive value here.

⚠️ Failure Case — mean, s1000 collapses on nl-NL

  • Same recipe as D (mean, s270, +0.35 % WERR) — only the training step differs (270 → 1000), so overtraining is the cause, not the reward design.
  • Failure is locale-specific: nl-NL DTER explodes (+274 % relative); hu-HU also collapses (+145 %), while en-US barely degrades (−0.96 %). Multilingual training under long-horizon RL forgets the weaker locales first.
  • Lesson: checkpoint with eval gate — for n=12 / s=1k configurations the sweet spot is <500 steps; do not blindly extend training.

📌 Findings & Recommendations

  • [Strong claim] fmt_judge + CER reward is the most reliable recipe in this sweep — +1.61 % overall WERR, all 18/18 datasets improve, only model positive across every locale.
  • [Claim] Among punctuation-only rewards, sum > mean > geo aggregator on this set (+1.38 % / +0.86 % / +0.95 %) — reward scale carries useful gradient and should not be normalized away.
  • [Claim] Small-batch (s=200) ReMax converges faster: best healthy models all use s=200 with checkpoints between step 140 and 309; the s=1k recipe collapses at step 1000.
  • [Novel finding] Per-locale gain is wildly asymmetric: hu-HU +6 %, en-US −1 %. RL on a multilingual ASR model is effectively a re-balancing operation, not a uniform lift.
  • [Novel finding] Dataset-type gradient: Dictation > Conversation > OnlineMeetings. Cleaner input ⇒ larger gain — consistent with punctuation/format reward being noise-sensitive.

⚠️ Limitations & Open Questions

  • Single eval metric: DTER bundles punctuation/format with lexical errors. Need WER + format-specific split to attribute the gains.
  • No statistical bands: sweep is single-run per checkpoint; 1.0 % deltas on cs-CZ & da-DK may be within noise. Re-run top-3 with seed variation.
  • Step coverage is uneven: only one ckpt for fmt_judge (step 200), while binary-adv has two (s140 + s200). Cannot tell whether fmt_judge has further upside.
  • en-US regression is unexplained — investigate whether the punctuation reward over-inserts on already-strong English transcripts.
  • Future: (1) extend fmt_judge to s=1k batch & later steps with early-stopping on en-US; (2) combine fmt_judge reward with binary-advantage aggregation (the two best healthy variants are orthogonal in design); (3) per-locale loss weighting to reduce the hu-HU vs en-US asymmetry.
